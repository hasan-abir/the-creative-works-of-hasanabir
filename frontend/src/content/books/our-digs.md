Twelve jabs at the world: Half inside the mind, half outside.
A poem per jab, nonetheless. A collection of poems.
Can poetry be anything more than an autobiography? Eh…
Yes. But how far from self can we get before it gets clinical?
Our Digs is here to tell.

“A tasteless lie you made up just to crack your father’s stone
Care; forget that it only somewhat worked.”

Within contemporary times: Your poet juggles between staying in proximity to his ruins and running away from it, which comes out as A Digger’s Secret,the first poem. A woman holds years of grudge against her husband, which makes her recall a special memory with someone else, hence Ollia Recalls. When a flowerpot in a city invites a dream, the dream invites a train, in particular, a Teal Train. A chicken vendor has chickens to sell, but what he sees up in an apartment looks just as sellable. Look Up in the Block. Your poet then remembers his lover as though she were his death, thereby confusing himself on what she meant to him, thereby What you. Sleep is necessary, but waking up, just as much, and when given the choice between the two, which one would children choose? Maybe, Some Sleep. Keeping his theme, your poet then remembers someone else, a boy, whose life he realizes has never been told about, but how should he speak for someone else? Being deeply affected, of course, and with his longest poem, To Pick Up to Put Down. A woman regrets trampling on someone else’s dream by winning their job, and so tries to goodheartedly mend her ways, but there are these eyes that pierce her through from above, the idea of Forward. Your poet then realizes, after all the remembering and descending, what is the worst he could make of love? Cute at Worst. A city is being reimagined, with binary colors, clearly begging for some serious “marketing,” and therefore, New Zebra City. Cultural purity is practiced, gatekept, and revisioned. So, considering such tendencies, is there really any harm in being impure, especially if it promotes growth? Your Bengali poet answers it with Pure as Bengal snow. And since you’re coming to know about your poet this closely, he would like to announce his love for himself directly in Loving Hasan.

As the poems display, Our Digs unravels in the fashion of an emotional journey, phasing in and out of the poet constantly, eventually landing where individuality is supreme, but not without that journey where individuals are still responsible for the world.
