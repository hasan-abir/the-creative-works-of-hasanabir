Four lessness: home, love, self, world.
A short story around each. A collection of short stories.
How the lack-ofs shape the protagonists, no,
how the lack-ofs are the very things that bring these protagonists to life.

“Time serves only the rich. I can wait, he thought, taking a seat on the ground in a lotus pose.”

Within 50 thousand words, within contemporary times: Homeless, a teen girl is only a half-orphan. Homeless, her father keeps his distance, and so does she. Their parallel lives lead to the story The Banal Pleas. Loveless, a man spends a weekend at home, in his head. Loveless, his wife spends it with her two new friends in their double bed. Their individual secrecy leads to the story, In a Chord. Blind, a married mother stays at home, absorbing complaints after complaints. Fine, her youthful daughter pushes through the complaints regardless. Her mother’s turmoil leads to the story High Knocks. In exile, a batch of islanders create a society in direct reaction to the tyranny that threw them out. Still in exile, the new batch of islanders no longer see what kind of a society put them on this island. Their glaring ignorance leads to the story, Ardor. And the overall chore nature of these stories leads to this book, Our Chores.

As the conflicts present, Our Chores is concerned with both the interior and the exterior, with both me and us. Readers will find under the English, under the stream of consciousness, more than just a glimpse of Bangladesh, including its serious poverty, its fragile human rights, and its sizable economic differences. Although generally hopeless, the protagonists do aim to show just how important self-empowerment is, whether having it themselves or not.
